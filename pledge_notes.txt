Organisations make dated promises: ship by a date, publish an audit. The promise is loud, the day it comes due is quiet, so keeping it on time is left to whoever remembers to look.

Pledge does the looking. pledge(commitment, source_url, due) records a promise, a public page where its truth shows, and a future deadline; a back-dated deadline is refused, and the author is bound to the caller. After the deadline, check(id), open to anybody, fetches the page and tells the round the deadline. A GenLayer round decides KEPT, LATE, BROKEN or UNCLEAR: only fulfilment on or before the deadline is KEPT; fulfilment after it is LATE, recorded as broken. The verdict accrues to the author, so record(address) builds a record of promises met on time and missed.

The deadline is part of the adjudication: refused if not future, injected into the round, and only an on-time verdict is written as kept. tests/pledge_rules.py proves this through the real methods. Proven on Asimov with a live board.
